# Outline
This project was inspired by two experiences: being in a family of violinists and studying to be an arts manager. Growing up, my brother and I played the violin, as my father is a violinist and so was my grandfather (and then his father made violins). This type of upbringing instilled in me many skills and disciplines that I am still reaping the benefits of today. Because of that, I believe that having access to art from a young age creates more empathy, critical thinking, emotional intelligence, and overall enhances quality of life.

As I complete my Master of Arts Management here at Heinz College, I learn that what I felt is not only correct, but backed by data. Americans for Arts has studies upon studies of how art affects people, communities, economies, people, and even crime rates. I want to show my audience that art isn’t just something to enjoy on your time off: it is a social need to have higher-functioning societies and communities. It is not just: “Arts heals”, it’s “Art creates better economies, decreases crime rates, and improves social well-being.”

Using data-backed research, I want to show my audience (in this case, I will be pretending that I am talking to private foundations without an arts portfolio with a mission to do social good) the importance of art in societies and why they should expand their reach and give money to the arts. I will do this by using a violin that was sold for $23 million dollars at a private sale, what that money could do, and the impact (social good) it will have on different communities. To do this, I will be using a Florida artistic after-school program called Miami Music Project Inc. The comparison will be between the impact the $23 million violin has on society versus $23 million in an arts program.

My potential one-sentence summary could be: Instead of a $23 million violin, why not a $23 million society?

# The data

This data is coming from various different sources. I found the information for the violin sale on two different websites. One was [ClassicFM](https://www.classicfm.com/discover-music/instruments/violin/stradivarius-sold-most-expensive-23-million/) which is also a radio for classical music. I knew the information would be accurate since their focus is everything that happens in the world of Classical music and not just sensationalist news. I also used [Amorim](https://amorimfineviolins.com/our-blog/the-5-most-expensive-violins-in-the-world-2025/), a company in Cremona, Italy, that is known world-wide for everything to do with violins. They deal in constructing the instruments, restoring them, and just knowing everything about the instrument,  in general. I trust in their information due to them being known worldwide. 

For my information on the Miami Music Project, I used [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/264084871/202641359349313619/full) for their tax documentation from 2025 and an [article](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/music/miamis-underserved-aspiring-orchestral-musicians-get-50k-federal-boost-40578129/) about the founder, the organization’s costs, and a recent endowment from the [NEA](https://www.arts.gov/). To find the Miami Music Project, I did use AI since I was not knowledgeable about music programs in Florida.

I believe that these four main sources are the only ones I will be using. However, I think I will more than likely add more to my story to give a bigger impact impact, so any more sources will be linked and explained here.

# Method and medium
For this project, I will be using Tableau, Canva, and [Flourish](https://flourish.studio/) (this is a new dataviz tool I have found while looking for easier ways to visualize my data)for my sketches. Tableau will be to design full-fledged data visualizations with concrete data while Canvas and Flourish will be used to both polish those visualizations (making them cleaner and more colorful) and also creating more artistic designs.

Additionally, I will be using Shorthand to make my final presentation for class. I think it's the most straightforward way to present my findings.
